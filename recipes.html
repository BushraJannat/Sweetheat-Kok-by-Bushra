COOKING SKILLS

Techniques Behind My Cooking

Good cooking is not only about having a recipe. It also requires technique, observation, timing and attention to detail.

This page focuses on the practical skills I have developed through years of cooking, experimenting and learning from experience.

How I Learned My Cooking Skills

Learning From My Mother

My first cooking lessons came from watching my mother in the kitchen. I learned the importance of preparation, seasoning and patience by observing how she cooked everyday meals.

I still use these basic habits when preparing traditional Bangladeshi dishes.

Learning From Recipes

After 2013, I started following recipes from a Bangladeshi recipe book. Trying different dishes taught me how ingredients, measurements and cooking methods work together.

I used this approach when learning to prepare dishes such as tehari, chicken roast and desserts.

Learning Through Practice

The biggest part of my learning came from cooking repeatedly. I learned to taste food, notice mistakes, adjust seasoning and understand how heat and timing affect the result.

I now use these skills whenever I experiment with familiar dishes or create something different.

Skills I Use in My Kitchen

Knife & Preparation

I learned preparation mainly through practice and by working alongside my mother and husband. Consistent preparation makes cooking easier and more organised.

I use this skill when preparing vegetables, meat and other ingredients before cooking.

Marinating

I learned that marinating gives ingredients time to absorb flavour. I pay attention to seasoning, spices and marinating time.

I use this technique especially when preparing chicken and other meat dishes.

Spice Layering

Through repeated cooking, I learned that adding spices at different stages can create deeper flavour.

I use this technique in Bangladeshi and Asian-style dishes where spices are an important part of the final taste.

Heat Control

Experience taught me that heat affects cooking time, texture and flavour. I learned to change the heat instead of simply following one temperature.

I use this when frying, simmering and cooking meat so that the outside and inside develop properly.

Texture Control

I learned to judge whether food should be crispy, soft, creamy, tender or firm by tasting and observing the result while cooking.

This is particularly useful when preparing fried food, desserts and dishes where texture is important.

Timing & Coordination

Cooking regularly taught me that different ingredients often need different cooking times.

I use planning and timing to bring several parts of a meal together without letting one part become overcooked.

My Cooking Process

I usually follow a simple process when I cook. It gives me opportunities to check the food and make adjustments instead of waiting until the end.

01

Prepare

I organise ingredients and prepare what is needed before I start cooking.

02

Season

I build the basic flavour using appropriate spices and seasonings.

03

Cook

I choose the appropriate heat and cooking method and pay attention to timing.

04

Taste

I taste the food during cooking rather than waiting until the very end.

05

Adjust

If something is missing, I adjust the seasoning, texture or cooking time.

06

Present

Finally, I think about portioning and presentation so the food looks inviting.

Skills in Practice

Beef Tehari

This dish gives me an opportunity to use spice layering, heat control and timing. I pay attention to how the spices develop before combining everything.

Chicken Roast

Chicken roast allows me to practise marinating, seasoning and heat control while focusing on tenderness and flavour.

Desserts & Baking

Desserts such as gulab jamun, ras malai, tiramisu and red velvet cake help me practise measurements, texture, timing and presentation.

Problem Solving in the Kitchen

Cooking does not always go exactly as planned. I have learned to observe the problem, understand what caused it and make a small adjustment instead of giving up.

Too Salty

If a dish becomes too salty, I try to balance the flavour by adjusting other ingredients rather than adding more seasoning.

Too Dry

When food becomes too dry, I consider whether more moisture or a lower cooking temperature is needed.

Not Crispy Enough

For crispy food, I pay attention to moisture, oil temperature and cooking time. Small changes can improve the final texture.

CONTINUOUS LEARNING

Skills I Am Still Improving

Cooking is a skill that can always be improved. I want to become more precise with baking, presentation and different cooking techniques.

I also want to keep experimenting with new ingredients and improve the way I combine flavours and textures.

My goal is not to cook perfectly every time. My goal is to learn from each experience and become a more confident and creative cook.
